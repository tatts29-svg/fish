Every phone on site is a tool store terminal.

A QR sticker on the store window. Scan it and the whole shelf is in your hand — 1,073 lines across 10 aisles, what is on the shelf, what is already out, the tag colour it has to carry and what has to come back with it. No app. No login. No data plan — it runs off the store’s own Wi-Fi.

The phone beside this is not a picture. It is the board, with the real catalogue inside it. Search it.

On the window, on the racks, on the crib room wall Scan → the board opens. Nothing to install, nothing to remember.

Same Wi-Fi, different doors

Everyone gets a board. Not everyone gets the same one.

One QR, one address, three destinations. The split is not a convention somebody remembers — the pages a worker can reach are named in a single list, and no page on that list is allowed to name a staff screen. It is checked on every build.

Anyone on site

The shelf

What we stock, which aisle, how many are on the shelf and how many are out. Photos, tag colour, kit list. This is the board in the phone above.

Crews & supervisors

Who is holding what

Their own company’s gear and their own people, with a worked gauge on each asset. A supervisor sees his crew. He does not see the store’s books.

Store staff — code

The stores board

Counting, racking, reordering, the fleet screens. Behind the store code, and the worker menu has no route to it at all — not a hidden link, no route.

No money crosses the store Wi-Fi. Not a rate, not a total, not even a zero — a zero is a claim, and on somebody else’s gear it is the wrong one. The reports that do carry money are encrypted under a manager code before they ever reach the folder a phone can open. There is no dollar figure anywhere in the board you are searching, because there never was one to remove.

The rule underneath all of it

A board that hides things without saying so is the one thing this refuses to be.

Lines can be held off the shelf — dead stock, a line that belongs to another site. Every one of them is listed on a page you can hold up, with the aisle it is held off and why. The totals say how many they left out rather than counting them as nothing, and a search that finds nothing says so plainly instead of showing an empty screen.

Try it: search the phone for something the store has never carried. It will tell you the shelf is 1,073 lines and none of them is that — which is an answer, not a blank.
