How the on-hire report works

One page. Three exports in, twelve tabs out. Powered by SiteIQ

This folder is a tool, not a system. It reads the SiteIQ exports you put in front of it and writes one Excel workbook and one email. It sends nothing, changes nothing on site, and does not need the internet after the first run.

The flow

SiteIQYou export three files — they land in Downloads.
RENTAL_STOCK what we own and where it is · SALES_STOCK consumables on the shelf and what's gone out · TRANSACTIONS every issue and return
▼you drag them into Data_SiteIQ\, over the top of yesterday's
REFRESH_THE_REPORT.batReads the newest of each, checks they all belong to the same job, and builds the workbook. About ten seconds.
▼your typed Labour and Cost tabs are lifted out of the old workbook and put straight back
<Job>_Onhire_Workbook_LATEST.xlsxTwelve tabs, ready to open or print. A dated copy is also filed under Reports\<today>\ so you keep every day.
▼and the same report laid out as an email, with the workbook attached
<Job> On-Hire Report — <date>.emlOpens in Outlook as a draft. Whole report in the body, workbook on the paperclip, no costs anywhere in it. Put the addresses on, read it, press Send. Nothing sends by itself.

What you actually do

1
Exports into Data_SiteIQ
RENTAL_STOCK, SALES_STOCK, TRANSACTIONS. STOCKTAKE too if you have it. Don't rename them — SiteIQ's own long names are fine, and the newest wins.
2
Double-click REFRESH_THE_REPORT.bat
A black window counts the tabs out. When it says DONE, close it.
3
Open the workbook, start on Cover — and open the .eml to send it
The workbook keeps the same file name every day, so a desktop shortcut never goes stale. The .eml is in Reports\<today>\ — double-click, address it, press Send.

The email

The same report, laid out to be read on a phone. It carries the position (what is out and who has it), who is holding gear, working hardest, not moving, consumables to watch and out the longest — then the full workbook on the paperclip for anyone who wants the line-by-line.

No costs in the email. None. Not the labour, not the cost breakdown, not a rate and not a dollar sign. That is deliberate — the email is the operational picture and it can go to anyone on site. The money stays in the workbook.

It is written as a draft, not a sent item. It arrives in Outlook with no addresses on it, waiting for you to put them on and read it through. Nothing sends by itself.

If anything is wrong it stops and says so in plain words — a missing export, an export from another job, no Python on the machine. It never builds half a report and leaves you to notice.

The two tabs nobody should overwrite

Coates Labour and Cost Breakdown are not built from the exports. Somebody sits down and types them, and there is no way to rebuild that from SiteIQ.

So the refresh never touches them. It reads them out of the previous workbook — values, formulas, dates, times, exactly as they were — and writes them back. Your original .xlsm is never written to at all; a clean workbook is built alongside it.

Keep your working .xlsm in this folder. That's where those two tabs are read from. If there isn't one, they come through empty with a note on them, ready to type into.

How the Usage Ratings are worked out

Both ratings are printed on the Cover tab of every workbook, so anyone reading the report can see how a recommendation was reached rather than taking it on trust.

Tooling is rated on TURNS — transactions divided by how many of that item are on site. One item that went out five times is working. Five items that went out once each are sitting there. Utilisation on its own cannot tell those two apart.

TurnsRatingRecommendation
noneNo UseReview / Reduce
under 1Low UseKeep Stock
1 to 2Good UseKeep Stock — or Monitor / Increase if 80% or more are out right now
2 or moreHigh DemandIncrease Stock

Consumables are rated on share of position sold — sales divided by sales plus what's left on the shelf.

SoldRatingRecommendation
nothingNo UseReview / Reduce
under 40%Low UseKeep Stock
40% to 80%Good UseKeep Stock
over 80%High DemandIncrease Stock

Putting it on another computer, or another job

Another computer: copy the whole folder across — all of it, not just the button. The machine needs Python 3; if it hasn't got it the button says so and tells you where to get it (python.org, big yellow Download button, and tick "Add python.exe to PATH" on the first screen). Excel is only needed to open the workbook, not to build it.

Another job: copy the folder, empty Data_SiteIQ\, put the new job's exports in, press the button. Every SiteIQ export carries the project it came from, so the report names itself correctly with nothing set up. JOB.txt is only there if you want to tidy up the names it uses.

One job's exports at a time. If a Gladstone export ends up in a Weipa folder, the refresh stops and names the odd file out. It will not quietly build a report off two jobs mixed together.
