 THE DATES - the one that matters
 --------------------------------
   Every on-hire item is now dated from the START of its issue
   transaction in SiteIQ, never from the stock record's ON_HIRE_DATE.
   That is what was putting August against gear that went out this
   week. Where the stock record and the transaction agree (most
   items) nothing changes; where they don't, the transaction wins.

   An item with no issue transaction inside the TRANSACTIONS export
   you pulled is shown as UNDATED - in the email, the pack and the
   workbook - and the report says how many and why. Pull TRANSACTIONS
   from the start of the shut and every item dates. The window the
   export covers is printed in the email footer, on the Cover and in
   the pack.

 NEW - THE COMPANY PACK (PDF)
 ----------------------------
   Reports\<today>\<Job> On-Hire Report by Company - <date>.pdf
   A cover with the position and a contents list that links to each
   section, then one section per company: their people A-Z, their
   gear longest-held first, item numbers, the day each item was
   issued, days out, and the consumables they drew this period. Each
   company is a bookmark in Acrobat. Attached to the email next to
   the workbook. One PDF per company in Reports\<today>\By_Company\
   as well, for forwarding a company just their page.

   Built by coates_pdf.py, a small PDF writer that now travels in the
   kit - nothing to install, no browser, no Acrobat needed to make it.

 ONE KIT, EVERY JOB (05 Nov 2026)
 --------------------------------
   The same kit now runs MMG and Weipa. JOB.txt carries the job's
   settings: engagement (fixed rate), shutdown_end (demob countdown
   and the gear-return push from four days out), tidy_names and
   acronyms. COMPANY_ALIASES.txt folds the spellings SiteIQ uses for
   one outfit - only what is written there, never a guess.

   The pack gains THE STORY on page 2: the brief in plain words, the
   team at the counter and their hours to date, how gear is moving
   (same-day returns, kept longer, still out), stocktake discipline
   (items sighted in the last three days, and by whom), customer-owned
   gear logged through the counter, and the standards behind the
   numbers. Every company that touched the counter gets a section,
   holding gear or not - at the end of a shut the activity is the
   story. A fifth dial on the charts page shows stocktake coverage
   when a STOCKTAKE export is in.

   The workbook gains a CUSTOMER GEAR tab - customer- and contractor-
   owned gear logged through the counter (fifteen tabs while a shut
   is ahead or under way, fourteen after). The
   email shows how gear is moving and an Own gear column, and says
   when demob is.

   4_MAKE_OUTLOOK_DRAFT.bat (optional) saves today's email as a NATIVE
   Outlook draft, so the To field has the address book behind it.

   A roster from a previous shutdown is recognised (its last day a
   month or more behind the report) and replaced with the job's seeded
   roster, with a note. MMG's roster and cost breakdown as sent on
   05 Nov 2026 are seeded, as corrected that day: fly in Sun 22 Nov,
   mobilisation Mon 23 and Tue 24 Nov, the shutdown from Wed 25 Nov -
   Thomas Maher on days, Cody Mitchel on nights - fly out Wed 2 Dec;
   $90 day / $100 night; tooling $1,847.81 and site services $50 a day
   from Wed 25 Nov to Tue 1 Dec, flights $1,500 each way. Labour reads
   off the roster: $23,160, and $39,444.67 all up; the Cover shows both.

   A tab that is still exactly an earlier version of the seed - nothing
   typed since - is updated to the corrected one, with a NOTE. A tab
   anybody has typed into never matches, so it is kept as typed.

   From then on the two typed tabs carry over from the kit's own
   LATEST workbook while the .xlsm still holds the old roster, so
   whatever you type into LATEST is what comes across each day (one
   NOTE says so). Before the shut starts, the story page shows the
   team as ROSTERED hours from the fly-in day rather than hours to
   date, so it never reads as zero people.

   On top of that, after an independent read of its changes:
   - The checks line is honest again. Five checks are fatal (workbook,

 NEW - THE SECOND OPINION (06 Nov 2026)
 -------------------------------------
   Two independent reviewers went over the kit - one read every line of
   the code, one rebuilt the kits from scratch and tried to break the
   outputs. What they found, and what changed:

   - A company still holding gear with no movement inside the export
     window was missing from Company Summary and from the "who is
     holding gear" tables, while the tiles and the pack still counted
     it. It now appears with zero movements - that is exactly the
     company to chase.
   - Two lines under one transaction number (a crew member taking three
     tools in one go) were counted once. The de-duplication now keys on
     the whole line, so a line that sits on both transaction sheets is
     still one movement and three tools are three.
   - The "cover and forecast agree" self-check compared a number with
     itself and could never fail. It now compares the typed Cost
     Breakdown total with the day-by-day profile the Forecast draws.
   - Running the kit twice on the same day left the morning's workbook
     as the Outlook draft's attachment. The copy is refreshed every run.
   - A typed roster only seeds a kit for the shut it was typed for.
     Next year's Weipa or MMG kit starts with blank typed tabs and a
     note, not last year's people and dollars.
   - The Dashboard's rating colours now follow the one palette (High
     Demand amber, Low Use slate), the same as the pack's donut.
   - The exports are parsed once per run instead of about ten times.
     Measured on today's exports the saving is under a second of a five
     to nine second run - housekeeping that matters more as the
     TRANSACTIONS export grows across a shut.
   - 1_CHECK_THIS_LAPTOP runs even when the kit is half unpacked - that
     is the case it exists to diagnose.
   - On a very big shut the cover's contents list tightens, then stops
     before the footer and says how many more companies have sections,
     so the asks beneath it are never squeezed off page one.

   The second reviewer rebuilt the kits from scratch and tried to break
   the outputs. No blockers. What it found, and what changed:

   - "This period" consumable draws now mean the transactions window.
     SALES_STOCK carries every sale the project has ever made, so the
     headline said 1,307 draws for a seven-day shut when 370 fell inside
     the window. The counted figure is the window's; the Consumable
     Transactions tab keeps every row; the Cover and DATA_SOURCES say
     how many were counted.
   - Customer-owned items (life jackets, drop mats) are on hire too but
     they are not Coates gear. The position sentence now reconciles
     "56 on hire" with "47 Coates fleet"; the company chase email says
     "on hire from the Coates tool store" and names how many are the
     customer's own.
   - Reports\<day>\By_Company is regenerated whole each run, so a
     company renamed by an alias cannot leave yesterday's PDF and draft
     beside today's; the self-check counts the files present.
   - The email attaches the dated workbook, not the undated LATEST, so
     saving the attachment daily never overwrites yesterday's.
   - One count of consumable lines (the utilisation lines the ratings
     are built on) on the tile, the dial and the donut.
   - Cover: the demob date comes from the roster when JOB.txt is silent,
     and "Engagement" says "not set in JOB.txt" rather than asserting.
   - Exports more than three days older than the report date are said
     in the email, on the cover and on the workbook Cover, not only on
     the console.
   - The undated ask fits the facts: when the undated items' stock dates
     are before the shut, it no longer asks for a pull that cannot date
     them.
   - Export times on the Cover and in DATA_SOURCES are SiteIQ's own
     request time, not the file's copy time. Phone layout lets every
     cell wrap. Plain-text email pluralises. The last age band is
     captioned. The section band keeps whole sentences. Button 4 picks
     today's folder, not the alphabetically newest. 3_GET_PYTHON copes
     with an apostrophe in the path. Only Windows' real Temp folders
     trip the "inside the zip" guard. A renamed job still finds its
     LATEST workbook.
   - Classic Outlook only: the .eml-as-draft and buttons 4 and 5 need
     the classic desktop Outlook; the .html is the fallback in the
     new Outlook. 0_START_HERE says so now.

   ONE THING TO EXPECT on the first run after this update: "since the
   last report" compares tooling moves only, and says "tooling moves",
   because the previous report counted every sale in SALES_STOCK as a
   draw. From the next report the full movements comparison returns.

 NEW - THE LOOK (05 Nov 2026, evening)
 ------------------------------------
   The pack is set in INTER, a proper typeface that travels in the
   kit's _fonts folder (SIL Open Font License). Only the characters a
   pack uses are embedded, so files stay small, numbers line up in
   their columns (tabular figures), and headings get a real bold. If
   the folder is missing the pack still builds, in Helvetica, and the
   run window says so.

   Item descriptions read like English everywhere they are displayed:
   "GLOVES,CUT 3,GFPR200,NON-VEND,SZ-11 MMG2025" shows as "Gloves,
   cut 3, GFPR200, non-vend, size 11". The site tag SiteIQ appends is
   dropped, SHOUTING CAPS are made readable, measurements, codes and
   acronyms are left alone (18V, 310ml, 20L, 2XL, GFPR200, LED). The
   transaction and stock tabs keep the raw text for lookups. Put
   tidy_items: no in JOB.txt to keep them exactly as SiteIQ writes them.

   Calmer colour. Amber now means "order more" (High Demand, Increase
   Stock, the two order flags); red is kept for gear out over 21 days
   and safety. A zero that is good news - items on hire 0 - reads green
   as "Nothing on hire" instead of a big black nought, and other zeros sit
   quietly in grey.

   Email: sections 3 to 5 show their top six lines with "another N are
   on the ... tab"; on a day with nothing on hire, section 2 becomes
   "Who moved the most gear" with the bar on movement instead of twelve
   empty bars and "nothing out" chips; the Do column is folded into the
   rating chip; consumables read "61 of 61 gone" with the bar; phones
   that honour it stack the tiles one above the other (Outlook ignores
   the rule and loses nothing).

   Pack: dial labels wrap instead of running into each other; the
   cover's contents list tightens when there are many companies so the
   asks always fit beneath it; small drawn icons on the cover,
   milestone and section tiles; the two busiest hours are shaded on the
   hour chart; a quiet company's zero tiles go to the end; inline bars
   in every section's consumables and own-gear tables.

   Workbook: Dashboard charts sized to their columns with legends
   underneath (no more clipped "High Der"), and the gear chart shows
   "who moved the most gear" when nothing is on hire; tab colours by
   group - orange for Cover, Dashboard and Forecast, charcoal for data,
   grey for the two typed by hand; the Forecast tab's Phase column
   carries the roster strip's colours; Company Summary groups each
   company's two lines under its total (the minus signs down the left
   fold them).

 --------------------------------
   While a shut is ahead or under way, the pack gains a LOOKING AHEAD

   milestones (fly in, counter opens, last shift, fly out, rostered
   hours), the roster as a day-by-day strip - travel, mobilisation,

   The workbook gains a FORECAST tab after the Dashboard, INTERNAL:
   six tiles (fly in, counter opens, last shift, fly out, labour off
   the roster, all up off the Cost Breakdown), live Excel charts of
   cost by day with the running total over it and where the money
   goes, then the day-by-day table - hours per person, each money

   the two typed tabs when the report is built, so a re-run after
