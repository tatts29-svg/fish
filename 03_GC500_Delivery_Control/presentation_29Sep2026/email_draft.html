Hi team,

I wanted to share something we've built for the Boost Mobile Gold Coast 500 that I'm genuinely proud of: GC500 Delivery Control. It's one live record for the whole build. Every delivery, every dollar and every hour of labour sits on one map that anyone on the job can open, from a phone in the ute or a desk at the branch.

I've attached a 12-page walk-through with screenshots. Here's the short version.

Where we're at (Tue 29 Sep, week 3 of the build)

  • 226 of 226 units due by today are recorded on site, and 67 of 67 references due in are there (63 complete).
  • 789 units across 10 trades on 198 references, with 4,342.5 m of fence work on the dockets.
  • 24 days to race day. The countdown is on every page, so nobody forgets.

The map is the star

The master layout plan is lined up on satellite imagery and on Google's photorealistic 3D model of the Gold Coast. That means every unit on the job is pinned where it really sits: 366 pins, checked against the master plan to within 0.1 m.

  • Search a reference, a name, an asset number or a map callout, and it flies you straight there.
  • Tap a pin to see what it is, where its delivery stands, its asset numbers, and who pinned it on the ground.
  • Every reference is getting photos, and they're pinned where they actually sit, dropped on site from a phone rather than guessed from the drawing. So far that's 100 photos on 37 references and 24 on-the-ground pins, so everyone knows exactly where they're going.
  • Navigate opens Google Maps directions to the exact spot, driving or walking. No more "it's near the second gate somewhere".
  • On the Ultra (4K) setting the 3D view is crystal clear, right down to street level.
  • It's quick on a phone. The first picture now takes 1.2 s on a desktop (down from 5.2 s), and nothing goes blank while you zoom.

Deliveries, drivers and installers

  • The Timeline shows every day, with one line per truck: the time, the carrier, what's on it and where it's going.
  • Each day has its documents ready to go:
    • a prefilled pre-start, linked to the SWMS;
    • a driver sheet and an install sheet, one A4 page per load, with photos, the plan close-up and a QR code that navigates to the drop point;
    • an email with the PDFs attached.
  • If something turns up short, it's flagged straight away, and the rest of the order can be booked onto another day without counting the money twice.

Costs and labour, told straight

  • $405,844 revenue ex GST against $218,803 of direct costs known so far. Those two are kept apart, stream by stream.
  • That's a $187,041 difference so far. It's clearly marked as not a margin yet, because crew wages are still in hours only.
  • Labour ticked per piece: $17,031 so far, with $6,517 on site ready to tick. The event labour scope is $55,817.
  • 2,168 hours are tracked, split into ordinary time, ×1.5, ×2 and race weekend.
  • It also shows where money could be missing: servicing isn't on any contract line (worth $85,102 at our card's pump-out rates), and 43 contract lines still need a rate. I'll follow both up with the branch.

Inventory and sub-hire

It shows what's on site by type: whose it is (Coates or sub-hire), what's numbered, what's spare and what's still to come. On a walk-around you type a sticker number, press Enter, and it moves on to the next location. It refuses a number that's already somewhere else. You can swap a Coates unit for a sub-hired one, or cancel an order, in a couple of taps.

The Showcase: day and night, in crisp Ultra 4K

The Showcase tells our build like race day. It's a 10-scene run with the Coates #26 around the Gold Coast circuit in 3D, past the grandstands and the crowd, in the day or at night with the city lit up. On the Ultra setting it's crisp enough for a 4K boardroom screen, so it looks the goods on the big TV. Every figure in it is read from the live record.

Broadcast: put the sound on for this one. It's a 35-part live race call, voiced like Supercars commentary with a lead caller and a colour commentator, and the crowd and a V8 underneath. It calls our build like the race is on right now. Honestly, it's pretty good to watch and listen to.

Coates FM: climb into the Coates Way machine, press RADIO on the steering wheel and you get a Coates FM segment with a radio host. It's tuned like a car radio, and the V8 drops back while it plays.

The machine itself turns our Coates Way into a V8 you can sit in. Best Service & Value is the centre cog, and there are 310 connected parts. You can start the engine, explode the powertrain, or follow the power path from the centre cog out to the wheels.
