## What it tells us

1. **Scale.** Last year's fence cost Coates about $335k. This year's forecast to job end is **$257k, $77k (23 %) under**
   — and the page already names what is not priced in that forecast: relocation 809 m billed by the hour (hours not
   known), removal 968 m, hoarding 137 m, flat feet 256 m, WPF 135 m, and the three demob weeks. Last year's figure says
   those gaps are worth a good part of $77k, not a rounding error. The forecast is not wrong; it is short by exactly the
   things it says it is short by.
2. **The bump-out alone was $23,586 last year** over three weeks. This year's removal and demob are not priced at all.
   That is a floor to plan for, and a figure to put to Advanced for this year's removal rate.
3. **Event Week was the biggest week last year ($95,812), 2.8 times this year's plan for it ($34,428).** Either last
   year's event-week PO carried scope this year's programme spreads elsewhere (Week 1 is this year's biggest, $54,523),
   or this year's event-week plan is light. One question for Advanced's programme manager.
4. **The shape matches.** Last year Week 1 and Event Week were the two big weeks; this year's programme has the same
   two as its biggest. The programme-times-rates method the page uses reads right against last year's actual spend.
5. **Weekly purchase orders.** Last year Coates raised one PO per programme week against one quote. If the branch does
   the same this year, each week's PO is the Rehire cost figure Finance posts (2126 and 2142), and the page's weekly
   accrual rows line up with them one for one.
